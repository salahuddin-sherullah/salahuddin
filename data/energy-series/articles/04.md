Transmission and Distribution (T&D) losses and unpaid bills have become a severe financial and operational burden on Pakistan’s power sector, costing ex-WAPDA DISCOs about Rs. 397 billion in FY25: Rs. 265 billion from losses above allowed targets and Rs. 132 billion from under-recovery. [11] These losses drive up electricity costs for consumers, impact industry competitiveness, and strain an already challenged power sector. But what exactly are T&D losses, and why are they so costly for Pakistan? This section breaks down the problem, explores its root causes, and discusses potential solutions.

## What are T&D Losses?

{{pdf:04.03}}

T&D losses occur in two main categories:

{{pdf:04.04}}

- **Transmission Losses:** Energy that dissipates as it travels from power plants over long distances to distribution networks. These losses are often technical and stem from infrastructure limitations.

{{pdf:04.05}}

- **Distribution Losses:** Caused by technical inefficiencies as well as electricity theft and equipment tampering, distribution losses occur within neighborhoods and near end-users.

{{pdf:04.06}}

In Pakistan, ex-WAPDA DISCO T&D losses reached 17.55% in FY25, against an allowed 11.43%. Recoveries also lag, with 96.6% of billed electricity payments collected. [11] The historical global loss benchmark was 8%, underlining the scope for improvement. [0][N]

{{pdf:04.07}}

## The Financial Impact on Pakistan

{{pdf:04.08}}

For consumers, T&D losses mean higher bills. Estimates published in 2024 suggested that T&D inefficiencies added approximately Rs. 5 to the cost of each unit, a burden passed to the public. [0][N] This increase exacerbates economic inequality, with high electricity costs disproportionately impacting low-income households. Additionally, T&D losses stifle industry competitiveness, as manufacturing and service sectors struggle with inflated power expenses.

The distribution challenge is becoming a fairness challenge too. A family with savings and a suitable roof can reduce its exposure to high tariffs. A tenant, a small shop or a household unable to finance panels has fewer choices. If network costs are simply pushed onto the units still bought from the grid, those consumers can bear a growing share. [110]

Selling less electricity is not the same as losing electricity through theft or faulty equipment. DISCOs need to distinguish lower purchases, physical losses and unpaid bills. Better metering can make that distinction clearer, while targeted support and access to shared solar can help consumers who cannot buy their own system. [110]

## Global Comparisons

{{pdf:04.10}}

Pakistan’s FY25 ex-WAPDA DISCO loss rate of 17.55% remains high against the historical international benchmarks. [11] The comparison published in 2024 reported India’s losses at around 15%, down from more than 25%, and Bangladesh’s at approximately 10.3%. It put the U.S. and Singapore below 8%. These historical benchmarks illustrate the scope for better infrastructure and management, but have not been refreshed to FY25. [0][N]

## DISCO Performance: A Tale of Two Extremes

{{pdf:04.12}}

Pakistan’s distribution companies (DISCOs) vary significantly in performance:

{{pdf:04.13}}

- **Best Performer:** IESCO recorded T&D losses of 8.61% in FY25, the lowest in this dataset. [11]

{{pdf:04.14}}

- **Worst Performer:** SEPCO, at 39.18% T&D losses in FY25, faces severe challenges in governance, infrastructure and theft prevention. PESCO’s rate was 37.15%. [11]

{{pdf:04.15}}

The uneven performance reflects disparities in local governance and infrastructure, highlighting the need for targeted solutions tailored to each region.

## Key Causes of High T&D Losses

{{pdf:04.17}}

1. **Aging Infrastructure:** Much of Pakistan’s transmission and distribution network is outdated, leading to inefficiencies and frequent breakdowns.

{{pdf:04.18}}

2. **Widespread Theft:** Unauthorized connections and meter tampering are rampant, particularly in residential areas, contributing significantly to distribution losses.

{{pdf:04.19}}

3. **Governance Challenges:** Many DISCOs lack the autonomy and skilled management necessary to improve efficiency, resulting in accountability gaps and operational inefficiencies.

## Learnings from Global Successes

{{pdf:04.21}}

Countries like India and Bangladesh have shown that strategic interventions can bring down T&D losses. For instance:

{{pdf:04.22}}

- **Smart Metering:** Smart meters allow real-time monitoring and help prevent theft. By 2024, smart-meter rollout in India had reportedly led to a 10–20% reduction in T&D losses. [0][N]

{{pdf:04.23}}

- **Governance Reforms:** Clear accountability structures, corporatization, and transparency in DISCO management have made a difference in countries facing similar challenges.

{{pdf:04.24}}

## How Can Pakistan Address T&D Losses?

{{pdf:04.25}}

1. **Strengthen DISCO Governance:** Enhancing management autonomy and setting strict accountability measures can create a more efficient operating environment for DISCOs. Moving toward corporatization and empowering management to operate independently can also support better results.

{{pdf:04.26}}

2. **Invest in Smart Technology:** Adopting smart meters and advanced monitoring technology can drastically reduce theft and improve billing accuracy. Pilot projects in Pakistan had reportedly reduced losses by 10% in some areas by 2024. Wider implementation could yield substantial improvements. [0][N]

{{pdf:04.27}}

3. **Shift T&D Management to Provincial Authorities:** Devolving control of DISCOs to provincial governments can improve accountability and enable solutions that reflect the unique needs of each region. Provincial oversight can allow for locally-driven reforms and resource allocation that are better aligned with on-the-ground realities.

{{pdf:04.28}}

Reducing T&D losses is critical for Pakistan’s energy future. Addressing these losses will not only reduce electricity costs for consumers but also strengthen the power sector, making it more sustainable and competitive. With targeted governance reforms, investments in smart technology, and local accountability, Pakistan can create a more efficient and resilient power network.
