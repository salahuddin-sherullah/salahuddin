# Pakistan and Counting – Energy Series: A data-driven narrative of Pakistan's power landscape (Updated Edition, November 2026)

*Original by Salahuddin, Caravan Media, December 2024 (2024 data). Updated to the latest available data as of 13 November 2026.*

The original story still holds, but several key numbers have changed. Pakistan's utility-scale generating capacity fell for the first time, to 41,121 MW at end-June 2025, after IPP contract terminations.\[1\] Power-sector circular debt was cut from Rs 2.393 trillion (FY24) to Rs 1.614 trillion (FY25) through a Rs 1.225 trillion bank refinancing. It then rose to Rs 1,675 billion "at the end of June" 2026, according to Energy Minister Awais Leghari's written National Assembly reply of October 2026. Rooftop solar now generates the equivalent of about 46% of grid sales.\[2\] Grid losses and weak bill recovery remain the biggest drain, at Rs 397 billion in FY25.\[1\]\[3\]\[4\]

## TL;DR
- **Supply shrank while off-grid solar grew.** Utility-scale capacity fell to 41,121 MW in FY25 (NEPRA) after 2,829 MW of plants retired. The Economic Survey's 49,651 MW (March 2026) counts 7,319 MW of net-metered solar. Renewables First's Pakistan Electricity Review 2026 puts distributed solar at about 38 GW, "equivalent to 93% of the country's" utility-scale capacity. It finds that "annual solar power generation is modelled at 51 TWh in FY24-25, or 46% of the grid-supplied electricity of 111 TWh."
- **Costs eased, but the structure did not change.** The national average tariff was cut by Rs 7.41/unit in April 2025, and circular debt fell to Rs 1.614 trillion. But capacity payments are still about Rs 1.9 trillion a year (CY2026–27 projections). DISCO losses plus under-recovery still cost about Rs 400 billion a year (Rs 265 bn + Rs 132 bn in FY25).
- **The policy landscape changed.**
  - Net metering was replaced by net billing on 9 February 2026. Dawn reports that new prosumers get "Rs11 per unit compared to Rs26 under the existing contracts."
  - A captive-gas off-grid levy took effect in 2025.
  - The competitive market (CTBCM) went live in January 2026, and its first 400 MW auction is under way.
  - FESCO, GEPCO and IESCO are being privatised.
  - The SBP raised its policy rate by 50 bps, from 11.5% to 12%, effective 27 October 2026, on a 9–1 vote (down from a 22% peak).

## 01 Understanding Pakistan's Electricity Ecosystem

**Installed capacity.** At the end of FY2024-25 (30 June 2025), utility-scale installed capacity was **41,121 MW** (NEPRA State of Industry Report 2025). That is down from 45,888 MW a year earlier, after 2,829 MW of plants were retired.\[1\] The 884 MW Suki Kinari hydropower plant partly offset the drop.\[1\]\[2\] This is the first year-on-year *decline* on record.

The Pakistan Economic Survey 2025-26 reports **49,651 MW** (end-March 2026). That figure includes **7,319 MW of net-metered solar**, so it is about 42,300 MW without it.\[5\]\[6\]\[7\] Against roughly 25,000 MW in 2015, utility-scale capacity is up about 64% (about 99% including net metering). *[Original: ~46,000 MW in 2024, +83% since 2015.]*

**Ownership shares** (Government 46% (mainly WAPDA), CPEC 17%, KE and IPPs 37%): **[NOT UPDATED – latest available: 2024]**. The mix has shifted since then, because the 2024-25 terminations hit private RFO and gas IPPs. The Economic Survey 2025-26 reports that 13 of 102 PPIB-facilitated IPPs (5,105 MW) have closed, and 90 IPPs (20,769 MW) remain operational.\[7\]\[8\]

**Installed capacity by province** (Punjab 43%, Sindh 35%, KPK 14%, Balochistan 8%): **[NOT UPDATED – latest available: 2024]**.

**Capacity by fuel** (NEPRA SIR 2025 data, compiled by Renewables First, FY25, 41.1 GW total):\[2\]
- Thermal: about 57% (23.2 GW)\[2\]
  - Coal: 18%
  - RLNG: 19%
  - Gas: 11%
  - RFO: 8%
- Hydel: 28% (11.5 GW)\[2\]
- Nuclear: 9% (3.6 GW)\[2\]
- Utility-scale renewables: about 7% (wind 1.8 GW, solar 0.8 GW, small plants 0.3 GW)\[2\]

**Demand vs. capacity.** NEPRA's SIR 2025 says demand peaked at just over **33,000 MW** in FY25.\[9\] Renewables First's monthly *system* peaks are about **28 GW in summer and 16 GW in winter**, essentially flat since FY22.\[2\] That leaves roughly 25,000 MW of utility capacity idle in winter. *[Original: ~30,000 MW summer, <17,000 MW winter, up to 29,000 MW unused.]* Thermal and nuclear plants in the CPPA-G system ran at an average **38.82%** utilisation in FY25, compared with 33.88% for the whole fleet in FY24.\[1\]

**Imported fuels.** RLNG (21.4%), imported coal (6.7%), RFO (0.4%) and electricity imports (0.3%) together supplied about **29% of utility-scale generation in FY25**.\[2\] This is a broader definition than the original's 22% (2024). Hydel, nuclear and renewables supplied **53.1%** of generation in July–March FY26 (Economic Survey 2025-26).\[7\]\[8\]

**Transmission.** The National Grid Company (NGC, formerly NTDC) had **25,950 MVA** of 500/220 kV transformation capacity in FY25. That is unchanged since FY23, with no new grid stations in FY25 (NEPRA SIR 2025 via Renewables First).\[2\] *[Original: ~26,000 MW vs 46,000 MW.]* Congestion has been measured:
- **64% of 500 kV and 59% of 220 kV transformers ran above 80% load**.\[2\]
- The Matiari–Lahore HVDC line was only **35% utilised**, because the Lahore North 500 kV substation was not commissioned until December 2025.\[2\]
- The Jamshoro–Rahim Yar Khan corridor is capped at **1,800 MW**.\[2\]

The named overloaded grids (Gatti, New Multan, Muzaffargarh, Lahore) and the FY23 transmission losses (2.4%, PKR 30.7 bn) are **[NOT UPDATED]**.

**Access and regional balance:**
- Village electrification (NTDC 84% vs KE 99.2%), 70–80% of the population on-grid, and the North/South split (generation 57/43, demand 77/23): **[NOT UPDATED – latest available: 2024]**.
- For context, about 7.3 million of roughly 40 million households had rooftop solar by FY25 (Renewables First).\[10\]

**Distribution companies.** There are still 10 DISCOs (9 public plus K-Electric), with TESCO reported separately. They now serve about **40.4 million consumers (FY25, excluding TESCO)**, according to NEPRA's Performance Evaluation Report (PER) FY2024-25.\[11\] *[Original: 38.2 million, FY23.]*

**Losses.** In FY25, ex-WAPDA DISCOs lost **17.55%** of electricity in transmission and distribution, against NEPRA's allowed 11.43%. That cost **Rs 265 billion**.\[2\] Unpaid bills cost a further **Rs 132 billion**, for a total of **Rs 397 billion** (NEPRA PER 2024-25).\[1\]\[3\]\[11\]\[12\] *[Original: ~16.3%, PKR 230 bn T&D, PKR 279 bn under-recovery, PKR 509 bn total in 2024.]* IESCO is still the best performer (8.61%). The worst is now **SEPCO at 39.18%**, ahead of QESCO (38.38%) and PESCO (37.15%).\[2\]\[11\]

**Institutions.** NEPRA is still the regulator, but the other bodies have been restructured. In April 2025, NEPRA transferred the system-operator and market-operator licences to the new **Independent System and Market Operator (ISMO)**.\[13\] Transmission now sits with **NGC**, and CPPA-G remains the DISCOs' buyer and agent.

## 02 Why is Electricity so Expensive

**Price trend.** The original's figures (price rises above 20% a year since 2020 vs a historical <8%; base tariffs doubled in under five years) are **[NOT UPDATED]**. The trend reversed in 2025:
- On 3 April 2025, the national average tariff was cut by **Rs 7.41/unit, from Rs 45.05 to Rs 37.64**. The industrial tariff fell by Rs 7.69, from Rs 48.19 to Rs 40.51 (Power Division tariff sheet, via Dawn and Geo).\[14\]\[15\]\[16\]
- Industrialists said part of this relief was withdrawn by July 2025, putting industrial rates back at Rs 34–35/unit (Profit).\[17\]
- NEPRA's FY26 power purchase price (PPP) fell to **Rs 25.98/unit**, from Rs 27.00 in FY25.\[18\]\[19\]
- CPPA-G estimates the PPP at Rs 25.32/unit for CY2026 and proposes Rs 27.31/unit for CY2027.\[20\]\[21\]

**International comparison.** The 2024 figures (Pakistan 21 cents/kWh vs India 8 and Bangladesh 7.5) and the 2021 figures (13 / 12 / 9) are **[NOT UPDATED – no authoritative 2025–26 comparison found]**. Indicative figures from the IEA's *Electricity 2025*, as reported in Pakistani media, put Pakistan's industrial rate at about 13.5 cents/kWh, vs 6.3 in India and 7.7 in China.\[22\]\[23\]

**Bill composition** (Generation 48%, Transmission 3%, Distribution 8%, Taxes 15%, Additional charges 26%): **[NOT UPDATED – latest available: 2024]**. The debt service surcharge, now **Rs 3.23/unit**, is earmarked to repay the 2025 bank refinancing.\[24\]\[25\]

**Imported fuels and price shocks:**
- Imported fuels supplied 47% of generation in 2021 and 22% in 2024, and about 29% in FY25 on a broader definition. The 2021–22 price spikes (oil +85%, coal +200%, gas ~+300%) are kept as history.
- A new shock came in 2026. The Economic Survey 2025-26 cites disruption around the Strait of Hormuz.\[7\] The SBP cited oil above $100/bbl when it raised rates in October 2026.\[26\]
- Dawn reported that mid-2026 NEPRA fuel adjustments put RLNG at Rs 45.93/kWh and RFO at Rs 45.25/kWh, which it tied to spot LNG buying during a Qatari force majeure.\[27\] This was **not independently verified**.
- The Farooq Tirmizi series on domestic vs imported fuels (2004–2024) is **[NOT UPDATED]**.

**RLNG.** RLNG supplied **21.4% of utility-scale generation in FY25** (28.7 TWh, down from 30.8 TWh in FY24).\[2\] The $54/MMBTU peak (August 2022) is kept as history. The current price and the rule of thumb that a 10% price rise adds 2% to unit costs are **[NOT UPDATED]**.

**Exchange rate.** The PKR's fall from 109 (2018) to a 306 peak (September 2023) is kept as history. The current spot rate is **[NOT UPDATED]**; CPPA-G's CY2027 base case assumes **Rs 279/US$**.\[20\]\[21\] The estimate that depreciation added ~Rs 14/unit (2021–2024) is **[NOT UPDATED]**.

**Interest rates.** The SBP cut its policy rate from the 22% peak to 11% (July 2025) and held it at 11.5% through mid-2026.\[28\]\[29\] Its Monetary Policy Committee then raised the rate by 50 bps to **12%, effective 27 October 2026**, on a 9–1 vote. Governor Jameel Ahmad cited "persistently elevated" global energy prices from the Middle East conflict (APP, 26 October 2026). Much lower rates have offset most of the original's estimate that high rates added Rs 4.3/unit; that estimate is **[NOT UPDATED in Rs/unit]**. They also made the bank refinancing cheaper.\[30\] The US Fed rate is **[NOT UPDATED]**.

**Excess capacity.** In FY25, about 41,100 MW of capacity faced a peak of just over **33,000 MW**, a surplus of about **8,000 MW** (NEPRA SIR 2025).\[9\] *[Original: 46,000 vs 34,000 MW in 2023; 12,000 MW surplus.]*

**Capacity payments:**
- FY26: NEPRA set capacity charges at **Rs 1.941 trillion** for ex-WAPDA DISCOs, or **Rs 16.67/kWh** of a Rs 26.34/kWh PPP (63%).\[31\]\[32\]
- CY2026: CPPA-G projects **Rs 1.923 trillion (Rs 14.86/unit)**.\[20\]
- CY2027: CPPA-G projects **Rs 1.914–1.962 trillion**.\[20\]
- FY25: NEPRA SIR 2025 puts the average capacity purchase price at **Rs 14.21/unit**.\[9\]

*[Original: Rs 2.1 trillion ≈ Rs 5.5/unit; ~Rs 0.7 trillion for unused power.]* The original's per-unit basis differs and is **[NOT UPDATED]**. The comparison of +84% capacity growth since 2014 vs +30% peak demand is also **[NOT UPDATED]**.

**T&D losses.** DISCO losses were **17.55% in FY25**, down from 18.31% in FY24. PESCO was at 37.15%, IESCO at 8.61%, and SEPCO was the worst at 39.18%.\[2\]\[11\] The original's cost estimate (Rs 1.15/unit) is **[NOT UPDATED]**.

**Recovery.** DISCOs recovered **96.6% of bills in FY25**, up from 92.44% in FY24. The cost of under-recovery fell to **Rs 132 bn**;\[11\]\[33\] the Power Division puts FY24 at Rs 315 bn.\[34\] *[Original: 86%, PKR 236 bn (2023).]* The estimate that 100% recovery would save Rs 2.3/unit is **[NOT UPDATED]**.

**Taxes:**
- PKR 860 bn in electricity taxes (about 9% of the tax base): **[NOT UPDATED]**.
- GST is now the **18% standard rate**, per 2026 coverage of the net-billing debate.\[35\] *[Original: 17.6%.]*
- Income tax (10–12%), the ~Rs 8/unit tax burden, and agriculture taxes (PKR 4 bn, FY23): **[NOT UPDATED]**.

**Circular debt.** The stock was **Rs 1.675 trillion at 30 June 2026**,\[36\] and the debt service surcharge is **Rs 3.23/unit** (see Section 05). *[Original: Rs 2.6 trillion; DSS ~Rs 1.5/unit.]*

**Industry.** The original's 11% drop in industrial grid demand (2021–2024) has **reversed**:
- Industrial sales rose 5% in FY25, from 27.7 to 29.1 TWh (NEPRA SIR 2025 via Renewables First).\[2\]
- They rose about 24% in July–March FY26, from 21,083 to 26,205 GWh (Economic Survey 2025-26).\[7\]\[8\] The main driver is that captive gas has been priced out (Section 06).

The textile export figure (−9.3%), the PIDE estimates (0.75% loss of competitiveness, 10% higher costs) and the captive estimate (~1,200 units, ~5,000 MW) are **[NOT UPDATED]**.

## 03 Excess Capacity

**Capacity payments.** Payments remain about **Rs 1.9–2.0 trillion a year**: Rs 1.941 trillion in the FY26 determination, Rs 1.923 trillion in CY2026 and Rs 1.914–1.962 trillion projected for CY2027 (52–57% of the PPP).\[20\]\[32\] *[Original: Rs 2.1 trillion a year.]* The renegotiations have **flattened rather than cut** the bill, because lower grid sales and new hydro and nuclear debt offset the savings. In November 2026, CPPA-G officials told NEPRA that capacity charges "do not appear to be coming down."\[20\]

**Capacity vs. demand.** Since 2015, utility capacity is up about 64%, to 41,121 MW (FY25). The grid peak has been flat since FY22.\[2\] Thermal utilisation was 38.82% (NEPRA) or 43% (Renewables First) in FY25.\[1\]\[2\] The original's Rs 1.05 trillion a year for idle plants is **[NOT UPDATED]**.

**Currency effect.** The original's illustration is kept: about Rs 750 bn at PKR 100/USD vs over Rs 2 trillion at PKR 300/USD.

**Who receives capacity payments.** The 2024 split (CPEC 36%, IPPs 15%, government/GENCOs 49%) against installed shares (46/17/37) is **[NOT UPDATED – latest available: 2024]**. The IPP share has fallen since the terminations.

**Chinese debt.** The ~$15 bn of Chinese power debt, and the estimate that reprofiling from 10 to 15 years would save $550–750m a year (Rs 2.5–3/unit), are **[NOT UPDATED – no confirmed reprofiling found]**.

**IPP renegotiations and terminations, 2024–26.** This replaces the original's "PKR 150 bn, ~Rs 0.6/unit."
- **Terminations.** The Economic Survey 2025-26 reports **13 IPPs (5,105 MW) closed**: 9 RFO plants (2,877 MW), 3 gas/RLNG plants (601 MW) and 1 multi-fuel plant (1,638 MW).\[7\]\[8\] NEPRA SIR 2025 records 2,829 MW retired in FY25 and estimates savings of "over Rs 900 billion over time."\[1\]
- **Renegotiations.** In March 2025, the Power Division told parliament that revised or terminated contracts with **29 IPPs and government plants** would save **Rs 3.498 trillion** over their remaining 3–20 years.\[37\]\[38\] The breakdown:
  - 5 early terminations: Rs 411 bn\[38\]
  - 8 bagasse plants: Rs 238 bn\[38\]
  - 14 IPPs under the 1994/2002 policies: Rs 922 bn\[38\]
  - Nandipur and Guddu: Rs 354 bn\[38\]
  - Haveli Bahadur Shah, Balloki and QATPL: Rs 1,808 bn\[38\]
- **Cabinet approval.** In January 2025 the cabinet approved the 14-IPP package at Rs 1.4 trillion over the plants' lifetimes, or **Rs 137 bn a year**.\[39\] Later official claims reached Rs 3.6 trillion (November 2025).\[40\]\[41\]
- **Reality check.** Dawn put the *annual* savings at about **Rs 117 bn**, possibly Rs 180–191 bn later.\[42\] On a grid base of about 111 TWh, Rs 117–137 bn is roughly **Rs 1.0–1.2/unit**. That is our estimate, not an official figure. The Power Division also secured about **Rs 260 bn in late-payment-interest waivers**, a one-off gain.\[43\] It now says the renegotiated IPPs have moved from take-or-pay to a **hybrid take-and-pay** model (National Assembly reply, October 2026).\[44\]\[45\]

## 05 Circular Debt

**Stock.** Energy Minister Awais Leghari told the National Assembly in a written reply (October 2026) that power-sector circular debt "amounted to Rs1,675 billion at the end of June" 2026. It was **Rs 1.614 trillion** in June 2025 and **Rs 2.393 trillion** in June 2024 (NEPRA SIR 2025; Power Division).\[12\]\[33\]\[34\]\[53\] *[Original: Rs 2.65 trillion (2.4% of GDP) in 2024, projected Rs 2.8 trillion by 2025.]* The projection was wrong: the stock *fell* by about Rs 780 bn in FY25.\[34\]

The Power Division attributes the FY25 fall to three things:
- better DISCO performance: Rs 193 bn\[43\]
- late-payment-interest waivers: Rs 260 bn\[43\]
- macroeconomic gains: over Rs 300 bn\[43\]

NEPRA says the fall came **mainly from fiscal measures**.\[2\]\[54\]

**FY26.** The stock rose about Rs 61 bn, above the IMF target of Rs 1.614 trillion.\[30\]\[53\]\[55\] According to Dawn, the Power Division blamed "the budget deduction of Rs98bn" by the Finance Ministry for the rise. Elsewhere it blamed a K-Electric shortfall, which it says added about Rs 194 bn (The Nation, 19 August 2026).
- Gross additions were **Rs 609 bn**. DISCO inefficiencies accounted for Rs 262 bn; interest fell to Rs 14 bn (from Rs 58 bn in FY25).\[53\]
- The stock peaked at about **Rs 1.84–1.89 trillion in February 2026**.\[55\]\[56\]\[57\]
- At June 2026 it comprised Rs 784 bn owed to producers, Rs 90 bn owed to fuel suppliers and **Rs 801 bn of circular-debt financing**.\[53\]

One National Assembly reply cites Rs 1.641 trillion for June 2025 instead.\[36\]\[45\] **The figures conflict; we use Rs 1.614 trillion.** The pre-deal stock (about Rs 2.4 trillion) was about 2.1% of GDP (Pakistan Banks Association).\[58\]\[59\] The current ratio to GDP is **[NOT UPDATED]**. Gas-sector circular debt reached **Rs 3.631 trillion** (June 2026), taking combined energy-sector circular debt above Rs 5.3 trillion.\[36\]\[45\]

## 06 Politics and Energy

**Global market structures.** The 1989 vs 2024 comparison (vertically integrated 215→72, single buyer 14→89, wholesale/retail competition 1→69; Global Power Market Structures Database) is **[NOT UPDATED]**.

**From single buyer to CTBCM (new).** Pakistan has formally begun moving away from the CPPA single-buyer model:
- 2020: NEPRA approved the CTBCM design.\[13\]\[67\]
- April 2025: system- and market-operator licences moved to ISMO.\[13\]
- October 2025: NEPRA approved the Market Commercial Code.\[13\]\[68\]
- **22 January 2026:** commercial market operations began.\[13\]\[69\]
- October 2026: uniform use-of-system charges of **Rs 6.23–19.62/unit** for open-access consumers were notified.\[13\]\[69\]
- **20 September 2026:** ISMO launched the RFP for the **first 400 MW wheeling auction**, open to consumers above 1 MW. Proposals are due **20 November 2026** and the auction is on **13 January 2027**.\[70\]\[71\]

The market is still very small relative to the system.\[13\]

**Fuel taxes and subsidies.** IFEM (~3.6%, ~Rs 10/litre) and the 2023 Rs 50/litre motorcycle subsidy (~Rs 450 bn a year) are **[NOT UPDATED]**. The FY27 budget proposes ending petroleum subsidies, and the petroleum levy reached **Rs 80/litre** by 20 August 2026 (Petroleum Division).\[72\]\[73\]

**Gas politics.** The historical 11% annual growth in gas connections, Article 158 disputes and the 2024 fertilizer gas subsidy (Rs 30 bn) are **[NOT UPDATED]**. For context, 149,908 gas connections were added in July–March FY26 (Economic Survey 2025-26), and the FY27 budget gives Rs 5.8 bn for urea.\[7\]\[74\]

**Captive power (policy changed).** The FY23 captive-gas subsidy (Rs 28 bn) has been replaced by an IMF-mandated **penalty regime**:
- The gas price was raised to **Rs 3,500/MMBtu** from 1 February 2025.\[75\]\[76\]
- The **Off the Grid (Captive Power Plants) Levy** (Ordinance from 30 January 2025, later an Act) phases in at 5%, then 10% (July 2025), 15% (early 2026) and 20% (August 2026).\[77\]\[78\]\[79\]
- Notified rates have included Rs 791/MMBtu (March 2025), Rs 238 (July 2025) and Rs 690 (August 2025). The IMF later revised the formula, bringing the levy to about Rs 500/MMBtu.\[75\]\[76\]\[80\]\[81\]
- The proceeds go towards lowering grid tariffs (~Rs 0.90/unit in the first phase). The FY26 target of Rs 105 bn was missed as captive gas use at SNGPL collapsed from 150 to **26 mmcfd**.\[76\]\[79\]\[80\]

The policy worked as intended: industry moved back onto the grid.\[82\]

**Thar coal.** The 175 billion-ton reserve is confirmed, and **five Thar coal IPPs (3,300 MW)** are running. Conversions of Jamshoro (660 MW) and Lucky Electric to Thar coal, and Thar coal blending at the three 1,320 MW CPEC imported-coal plants, are in progress (Economic Survey 2025-26).\[7\]\[83\]

**Residential losses** (T&D 16.4%, under-recovery 7%): **[NOT UPDATED]**.

**Hydropower.** Potential is 60,000 MW. Developed capacity is now about **11,500–11,615 MW**, up from 10,600 MW after **Suki Kinari (884 MW)** began commercial operation in November 2024.\[1\]\[2\]\[7\] Status of major projects:
- **Mohmand (800 MW):** about 50% complete; generation expected in early 2028 (WAPDA, January 2026).\[84\]
- **Dasu Stage-I (2,160 of 4,320 MW):** first power around December 2027 (Business Recorder), with large cost overruns reported.\[85\]\[86\]
- **Diamer-Bhasha (4,500 MW):** about 20% physical progress at June 2025; main-dam concreting starts in 2026; dam targeted for 2030 (Dawn).\[85\]\[87\]\[88\]
- **Tarbela 5th Extension (1,410 MW):** first unit due April 2027, now at risk after a 2026 rockfall.\[89\]
- **Nuclear:** C-5 (1,200 MW) is due in 2030-31.\[7\]

**Agriculture.** The 63% recovery figure is **[NOT UPDATED]**. Agricultural grid sales fell 47%, from 11 TWh (FY22) to 5.8 TWh (FY25) (Renewables First), and a further **42.3%** in July–March FY26 (Economic Survey),\[2\]\[8\] as solar tube-wells replaced grid and diesel pumps.\[2\]\[7\] Progress on the tube-well solarisation programme is **[NOT UPDATED]**.

## 07 Growing Residential Demand

**Utilisation and peak.** The original's average utilisation (23,000 MW of 46,000 MW) is **[NOT UPDATED in MW]**. Thermal and nuclear utilisation was 38.82% in FY25.\[1\] System peak was about 28 GW in summer and 16 GW in winter;\[2\] NEPRA's demand peak was above 33,000 MW.\[2\]\[9\]

**Average tariff.** *[Original: Rs 54/unit.]* After April 2025, the national average was Rs 37.64/unit and the domestic average about Rs 34.37/unit (Power Division via Express Tribune).\[14\]\[90\] Households using more than 300 units pay about Rs 48.46/unit.\[90\]

**Solar imports.** *[Original: 13,000 MW in H1 2024.]* Cumulative panel imports:
- about **48 GW** by June 2025\[91\]\[92\]
- **50.6 GW** by September 2025\[93\]
- about **54 GW** by March 2026\[2\]\[10\]
- **55.7 GW** by May 2026\[94\]

Imports are slowing. January–May 2026 imports were 4,574 MW, against 11,781 MW a year earlier (−61%).\[94\] January–July 2026 imports were 6.78 GW (Pakistan Solar Association).\[92\]

**Net metering → net billing (policy changed):**
- March 2025: the ECC cut the buyback rate for *new* users to **Rs 10/unit**.\[95\]\[96\]
- **9 February 2026:** NEPRA notified the **Prosumer Regulations 2026** (SRO 251(I)/2026), replacing net metering with **net billing**. Dawn reports that exported power is paid at the "National Average Energy Purchase Price (NAEPP) of around Rs10 per unit." New prosumers get "Rs11 per unit compared to Rs26 under the existing contracts." Power drawn from the grid is billed at the full retail tariff plus 18% GST. Agreements now run 5 years instead of 7.\[35\]
- After the Prime Minister intervened, a February 2026 amendment **protected the ~466,000 existing net-metered users** on their old terms until their contracts expire.\[97\]\[98\]

IEEFA's April 2025 briefing note ("Net Metering Reforms and Grid Challenges Amid Pakistan's Solar Rise") finds that "reducing the buyback rate to PKR10/kWh further increases the payback period to 4.5 years" for a 10 kW system. Under net billing at that rate, it puts payback at up to 6.5 years, compared with the original's 2–3 years.

**Vehicles and transport.** The original's vehicle and transport figures are **[NOT UPDATED – latest available: 2024]**:
- 30 million motorcycles and ~6 million cars, projected at 40m and 6.7m by 2030
- transport's $13 bn imported-fuel bill and 30% of emissions
- 4,500 MW to electrify all transport (cars 1,400 MW; trucks/tractors 1,550 MW; motorcycles 1,300 MW; buses/SUVs 250 MW)

For context, transport used **82.5%** of petroleum products in July–March FY26 (11.26 million tonnes, +6.7%; Economic Survey).\[7\]

**EV costs.** Retrofit and EV prices (motorcycle retrofit Rs 65,000–130,000; car retrofit ~Rs 2.7m; e-bikes Rs 200,000–600,000; EV cars Rs 5m to >9m) and the NEV Policy 2025-30 details are **[NOT UPDATED]**. The FY27 budget allocates **Rs 8 bn** to the EV scheme.\[74\]\[99\]

**Residential share.** Households took **50.8% of electricity in FY25** (57,455 GWh) and **47.5% in July–March FY26** (39,472 GWh), per the Economic Survey 2025-26.\[7\]\[8\] The global benchmark of 28% is kept. The share is falling because households are self-supplying with solar.\[100\]

**Household gas and electrification.** The original's 17 million gas-connected households is **[NOT UPDATED]**. The Economic Survey reports **over 10.9 million gas consumers**;\[7\] the definitions may differ. The $1.3 bn household gas cost and the electrification estimates (cooking 2,100 MW + heating 2,100 MW + water heating 700 MW = 4,900 MW; 9,400 MW including transport) are **[NOT UPDATED]**.

**Winter packages.** Consumption-based winter relief packages ran in 2024-25. Renewables First credits them for K-Electric's FY25 sales recovery.\[2\] The 2025-26 package details are **[NOT UPDATED]**.

**Impact of solar on grid demand (new):**
- **Generation.** Renewables First's Pakistan Electricity Review 2026 reports that "annual solar power generation is modelled at 51 TWh in FY24-25, or 46% of the grid-supplied electricity of 111 TWh." Ember and Renewables First ("The solarisation of Pakistan's energy economy", June 2026) find that "28% of Pakistan's electricity generation was distributed solar in FY25, up from 10% in FY23." They put distributed solar at 32% of electricity supply. They also find total demand rose 21% in two years, all of it met by solar, while grid generation fell 3%.
- **Grid sales.** Sales fell from **125 TWh (FY22) to 111 TWh (FY25)**. They grew just 1.7% in FY25 despite 5.3% consumer growth, and utility-scale generation fell for a third straight year, to 135 TWh.\[2\]
- **Demand in 2026.** Generation fell **9.6% YoY in April 2026** as net-metered output hit a record (Arif Habib, NEPRA data). Grid demand recovered by July 2026, as industry returned to the grid and evening peaks rose.
- **Batteries.** About 4.6 GWh of battery storage was imported in 2025 (+220%, Renewables First).\[10\] That threatens evening grid demand next.

**Implication.** About Rs 1.9 trillion a year in fixed capacity costs is now spread over flat or shrinking grid sales. That is the core "utility death spiral" risk, and the main reason for the switch to net billing.

## Caveats
- **"Installed capacity" figures are not comparable.** NEPRA's 41,121 MW is utility-scale only; the Economic Survey's 49,651 MW includes net metering. Likewise, NEPRA's peak (>33,000 MW) is computed demand, while Renewables First's ~28 GW is the peak the system actually served.
- **Circular-debt baselines conflict.** End-FY25 is Rs 1.614 trillion in most official figures but Rs 1.641 trillion in one National Assembly reply. FY24 is restated to Rs 2.393 trillion (the original used ~Rs 2.65 trillion).
- **The FY24 loss split was revised.** Later sources put T&D at about Rs 276 bn and under-recovery at Rs 315 bn, versus the original's Rs 230 bn and Rs 279 bn. The original figures are kept.
- **IPP "savings" are official, undiscounted lifetime claims.** Realised savings appear to be about Rs 117–137 bn a year.
- **Distributed-solar figures are modelled** from customs data (Renewables First, Ember), not official statistics.
- **Mid-2026 fuel-cost spikes** come from secondary reports and were not verified.
- **No FY26 NEPRA data yet.** Neither the State of Industry Report 2026 nor the FY2025-26 Performance Evaluation Report was available at the time of this update. Sources are named inline; a separate reference list is omitted.
